    How to work out a discount

    A discount is a share of the price taken off. To find the sale price, turn the percent into a decimal and subtract it from 1, then multiply by the original price.

    Sale price = price × (1 − discount ÷ 100)

    For a $120 jacket at 25% off: 120 × 0.75 = $90. You save $30.

    To find how much you save, multiply the price by the discount as a decimal: 120 × 0.25 = $30.

    Find the percent off from two prices

    If a shop shows only the old and new price, divide the amount saved by the original price.

    Percent off = (original − sale) ÷ original × 100

    An $80 pair of shoes on sale for $56 is (80 − 56) ÷ 80 = 0.30, so 30% off.

    Stacked discounts are not added together

    "30% off, then an extra 20% at checkout" is not 50% off. The second discount comes off the already reduced price.

    On $100: 30% off leaves $70. Another 20% off $70 is $14, so you pay $56. That is 44% off in total, not 50%.

    Use the Extra % off field above to check any stacked deal.

    Sales tax and discounts

    In most places tax is charged on the discounted price, not the original. The calculator applies tax last, after every discount, which matches how shops ring up a sale.

    Questions

    How do I calculate 20% off?

    Multiply the price by 0.8. A $45 item at 20% off costs 45 × 0.8 = $36.

    Is 40% off then 10% off the same as 50% off?

    No. You pay 0.6 × 0.9 = 0.54 of the price, which is 46% off.

    How do I find the original price from a sale price?

    Divide the sale price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). If $63 is the price after 30% off, the original was 63 ÷ 0.7 = $90.

    Does the calculator store what I type?

    No. Everything is worked out in your browser. Only your currency choice is remembered on your device.
